In Switzerland, open fires are permitted in many places — but the rules vary by canton, season, and current conditions. Ignoring them can result in heavy fines and, more importantly, a wildfire.

## General rules

- **Forest fires are prohibited** — fires are never allowed inside a forest or within 10 m of the forest edge, unless you are at a designated fire pit.
- **Designated fire sites (Feuerstellen)** — these are marked spots with a fire ring or grill. Using one is always the safest option and is permitted even when a fire ban is in place in some cantons.
- **Open-land fires** — allowed in many cantons on private or public open land, subject to the fire-danger level.

## Fire-danger levels

Switzerland uses a five-level fire-danger scale, published daily at [waldbrandgefahr.ch](https://www.waldbrandgefahr.ch):

| Level         | Colour   | Open fire allowed?    |
| ------------- | -------- | --------------------- |
| 1 — Low       | Green    | Yes                   |
| 2 — Moderate  | Yellow   | Yes, with care        |
| 3 — High      | Orange   | Designated sites only |
| 4 — Very High | Red      | Forbidden             |
| 5 — Extreme   | Dark Red | Forbidden             |

## Cantonal differences

Some cantons (e.g. Valais, Ticino) have stricter rules and publish their own fire bans. Always check your specific canton's website in addition to the national level.

## Summary

> **Before every fire:** check waldbrandgefahr.ch, confirm no cantonal ban is active, and verify you are at least 10 m from the nearest forest edge.
