Even if the weather looks perfect and you have found a great spot on the map, a fire ban can change everything. Always check on the day of your planned bonfire.

## Where to check

- **[waldbrandgefahr.ch](https://www.waldbrandgefahr.ch)** — the official national fire-danger portal. Enter your location to get the current danger level and any active bans.
- **Your canton's website** — search for your canton + "Feuerverbot" (fire ban). Many cantons list active bans in their news or safety sections.
- **Local municipality** — for areas near villages or on communal land, a quick call to the Gemeindeverwaltung (municipal office) can confirm local rules.

## What the levels mean for you

- **Level 1–2:** Open fires permitted. Still follow all standard safety rules.
- **Level 3:** Only designated fire pits (Feuerstellen) with a grill are allowed. No ground fires.
- **Level 4–5:** All open fires forbidden, including designated pits in many cantons. Postpone your trip.

## What to do if a ban is declared after you arrive

If a ban is declared while you are already on site, extinguish the fire immediately using the full extinguishing procedure covered in Module 6. A ban can come into effect with little warning during high-risk periods.

> **Rule of thumb:** if in doubt, don't light it. The forest will be there next weekend.
