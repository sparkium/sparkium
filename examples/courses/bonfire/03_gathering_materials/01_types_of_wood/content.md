Not all wood burns the same way. Choosing the right type makes the difference between a roaring fire and a smoky, frustrating evening.

## Hardwood vs. Softwood

| Type         | Examples          | Burns                   | Best for       |
| ------------ | ----------------- | ----------------------- | -------------- |
| **Hardwood** | Beech, oak, ash   | Slow, hot, long-lasting | Main fuel logs |
| **Softwood** | Spruce, pine, fir | Fast, with more smoke   | Kindling       |

## What to look for

- **Dry wood (seasoned)** — wood should have been stored dry for at least 6–12 months. Tap two pieces together: a hollow knock means it is dry.
- **No rot** — soft, crumbling wood produces little heat and lots of smoke.
- **Untreated** — never burn painted, varnished, or pressure-treated wood. It releases toxic fumes.

## What to avoid

- **Green (freshly cut) wood** — high moisture content produces thick smoke and little heat.
- **Driftwood** — salt-impregnated wood burns with toxic chlorine compounds.
- **Plywood and chipboard** — contain glues and resins that are harmful when burned.

> **Swiss tip:** Beech (*Buche*) is the most widely available hardwood in Switzerland and an excellent choice for a sustained, hot fire.
