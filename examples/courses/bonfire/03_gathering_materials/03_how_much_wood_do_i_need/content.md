Gathering too little wood means your fire dies early; too much leaves waste and unnecessary impact on the environment. Use these guidelines to get it right.

## The three-layer model

Every fire needs three categories of fuel in the right proportions:

| Layer         | Size                  | Amount for a 3-hour fire          |
| ------------- | --------------------- | --------------------------------- |
| **Tinder**    | matchstick-thin       | 1–2 handfuls                      |
| **Kindling**  | pencil-thick          | a bundle the size of your forearm |
| **Fuel logs** | wrist-thick or larger | 8–12 pieces, 30–40 cm long        |

## Practical rules of thumb

- **1 hour of fire ≈ 2–3 fuel logs** of beech or oak (hardwood).
- Softwood burns roughly twice as fast, so double the quantity.
- Always collect **30–40 % more** than your estimate — damp conditions and wind can halve your expected burn time.

## Sourcing responsibly

- Collect **only dead, fallen wood** from the ground. Never cut branches from living trees.
- Spread your collection over a wide area rather than stripping one patch bare.
- Leave large rotting logs in place — they are vital habitat for insects and fungi.

> **Tip:** The "arm-bundle" test: if you can carry all your wood in one armload, you probably have enough for a 2-hour social fire.
