Tinder is the fine, highly combustible material that catches the first spark or flame and passes it on to your kindling.

## Natural tinder

- **Birch bark** — contains natural oils that ignite easily even when slightly damp; peel only from fallen or dead trees.
- **Dry grass and leaves** — bundle loosely into a "bird's nest" shape.
- **Dry pine needles** — plentiful in Swiss forests; collect from the ground, not from living branches.
- **Fungi (amadou)** — dried horse-hoof fungus (*Fomes fomentarius*) is a traditional Swiss tinder.

## Shop-bought tinder

- **Fire-starter cubes** — reliable and weatherproof; a good backup.
- **Wax-coated cotton pads** — easy to make at home and very effective.
- **Char cloth** — charred natural fabric that catches even a weak spark.

## How much do you need?

A handful roughly the size of a tennis ball is enough to get most fires going. Always gather more than you think you need — damp conditions or wind can consume tinder quickly.

> **Tip:** Store tinder in a sealed zip-lock bag in your pack to keep it dry until you need it.
