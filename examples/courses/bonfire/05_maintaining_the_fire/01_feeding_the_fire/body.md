A well-maintained fire burns efficiently, safely, and enjoyably. The key is steady, patient feeding — not large dumps of wood.

## When to add wood

- Add fuel when the flames drop noticeably but you still have a good bed of glowing coals.
- **Don't wait** until the fire is nearly out — reviving a cold fire wastes time and tinder.
- **Don't overfeed** — a fire buried in unburned logs smothers and smokes.

## How to add wood safely

1. Use tongs or gloves — never reach bare-handed over an active fire.
2. Add one or two pieces at a time, placed gently at the edges of the coal bed.
3. Lay new logs **parallel** to existing ones, with a finger-width gap for airflow.
4. Step back and let the fire draw in air naturally.

## Hardwood vs. softwood timing

- Use **softwood kindling** to boost a flagging fire quickly.
- Switch to **hardwood fuel logs** for sustained heat and long-lasting coals.
- Mixing both gives you control over burn rate.

## Signs your fire needs attention

| Sign                        | Action                                |
| --------------------------- | ------------------------------------- |
| Lots of smoke, little flame | Improve airflow; split logs are drier |
| Flames dying, no coals      | Add kindling first, then fuel         |
| Fire growing too large      | Stop adding wood; let it burn down    |
