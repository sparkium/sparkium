Bigger is not better. A fire that fits within your cleared pit, with flames no taller than about 1 metre, is far safer and easier to manage than a roaring blaze.

## Why size matters

- Large fires produce more airborne sparks that can travel far downwind.
- They are much harder to extinguish quickly if conditions change.
- They create intense radiant heat that keeps people (and your water supply) uncomfortably far away.

## Keeping the fire in check

- **Don't add large logs** all at once — add one piece at a time and observe the effect.
- **Spread the wood** slightly if the fire is burning too intensely. A more spread-out fire burns less fiercely.
- **Let it burn down** if it grows beyond your pit. Stop feeding it and let the existing wood reduce.

## Maximum recommended size

For a typical social fire in Switzerland:

- **Diameter:** no larger than your cleared pit (max ~1 m)
- **Flame height:** 0.5–1 m under calm conditions
- **Distance from seated guests:** at least 2–3 m

## Wind changes everything

A fire that was perfectly manageable in calm conditions can become dangerous in a sudden gust. If wind picks up:

1. Stop adding wood immediately.
2. Reduce the fire by spreading coals with a shovel.
3. Be ready to extinguish if sparks start flying beyond the cleared zone.
