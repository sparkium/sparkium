The site should look the same — or better — than when you arrived. Future visitors, hikers, and wildlife depend on it.

## Ash and charcoal disposal

- **Cold, wet ash** can be scattered thinly over a wide area in a forest (it is a natural material and biodegrades quickly).
- **Charcoal chunks** that have not fully burned should be taken home in a sealable container and disposed of in household waste — they break down very slowly.
- **Never bury ash or charcoal** in a pile — concentrated deposits smother soil organisms.

## Restore the ground

- Fill any depression you dug with the mineral soil you removed.
- Scatter the cleared leaf litter and debris back over the area naturally.
- If you used rocks to create a fire ring, return them to roughly where they came from.

## Take your waste

- All food packaging, tin foil, bottle caps, and other non-natural items must be carried out. **Nothing goes into the fire pit.**
- Check the surrounding area in a 5-metre radius for any rubbish that has blown away.

## A final look

Before leaving, turn around and look at the site from a few metres away. Ask yourself: *would I know a fire had been here?* If yes, do more.

> Practising leave-no-trace keeps Switzerland's forests and open spaces available for open fires. It's how we protect the privilege.
