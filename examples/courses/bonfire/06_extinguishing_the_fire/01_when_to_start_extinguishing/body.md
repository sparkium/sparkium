Extinguishing a fire properly takes time — typically 20–30 minutes for a medium-sized bonfire. Start early enough that you are not rushing in the dark.

## Plan your end time before you start

Decide in advance when you want to leave the site, then count backwards:

- **30 minutes** for extinguishing and cooling
- **15 minutes** for cleaning up the site
- **Buffer** for packing and the walk back

Begin extinguishing **at least 45 minutes** before you need to leave.

## Signals it's time to stop

| Situation                           | Action                                                                |
| ----------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Darkness approaching                | Begin extinguishing — never leave a fire to burn unattended overnight |
| Weather deteriorating (wind rising) | Stop immediately and extinguish                                       |
| Running low on water                | Begin extinguishing while you still have enough to do it safely       |
| Everyone is ready to leave          | Start the process 45 minutes before departure                         |

## The golden rule

> **Never leave a fire before it is completely cold to the touch.** A fire that looks out can smoulder underground for hours and reignite.
