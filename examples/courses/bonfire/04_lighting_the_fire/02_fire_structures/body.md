How you stack your wood determines how your fire starts, burns, and behaves. Three structures cover almost every situation.

## Teepee

**How:** Lean kindling sticks against each other in a cone shape around a tinder bundle in the centre. Add progressively larger sticks on the outside.

**Best for:** Starting the fire quickly. The teepee concentrates heat upward and gets kindling burning fast.

**Limitation:** Collapses as it burns — transition to a log cabin or star fire once established.

## Log Cabin

**How:** Place two fuel logs parallel, then two more perpendicular on top (like a square frame). Repeat, progressively narrowing. Place tinder and kindling inside.

**Best for:** A long-lasting, steady fire that produces good coals for cooking.

**Limitation:** Slower to ignite than a teepee; needs dry, well-seasoned wood.

## Star Fire

**How:** Arrange 4–6 large logs like the spokes of a wheel, all pointing at the centre. Push them inward as they burn.

**Best for:** Conserving wood over a very long session (overnight fires). Easy to manage with minimal effort.

**Limitation:** Produces less intense heat; not ideal for cooking.

## Recommended approach

1. Start with a **teepee** of tinder and kindling to establish the fire.
2. Once burning well (10–15 minutes), transition to a **log cabin** for a steady social fire.
3. For extended sessions, shift to a **star fire** to reduce how often you need to add wood.
